1. THE PITCH
================================================================================

One sentence:
  You lead a fragile caravan of survivors, people you come to know and lose,
  across a dying state to catch the last boat out, trading time, food and
  lives at every stop.

It plays like:
  Organ Trail  (the journey: a road, a clock, supplies running out, random
                events, gravestones)
  + XCOM       (the fights: individual people, hit chances, directional
                cover, flanking)
  + Dead of Winter (the people: a big cast of distinct characters with
                special abilities and personalities, some strong, some weak)
  + RimWorld   (the story: small scenes that fire based on who is alive, how
                they feel about each other and what is happening, so every
                run tells a different story)

The tone is gritty horror with dark humour. It's not a power fantasy: you are
always short of something.

Pillars (every feature must serve at least one):
  1. Every person matters. Names, faces, quirks, relationships. A death
     changes the group and the story.
  2. Scarcity is the clock. Food, fuel, ammo, meds and days all drain
     together. Every choice spends one thing to save another.
  3. Quiet is safety, noise is death. Guns solve a problem now and create a
     bigger one two turns later.
  4. Surprise, not puzzles. You don't know what's in the next house. You
     plan for it; you can't solve it.
  5. Short, readable fights on a tiny screen: 10-15 minutes each.


================================================================================
2. WHAT THE OWNER WANTS (decision log)
================================================================================

These were decided by the owner in the design interview. Don't change them
without asking.

Feel and story
  - Organ Trail journey, gritty horror. A story that runs across time: the
    whole cast carries from mission to mission.
  - A heavier story than Advance Wars. Key characters can die, and the story
    adapts.
  - Emergent story built into the game as code and data (no AI inside the
    game). Hundreds of small scenes ("storylets") checked by a database so
    they can never contradict the game state.
  - The Campfire Chronicle: the game keeps a journal of your run.

People
  - Individuals, not squads. Each person has a background, a personality,
    attributes, skills and a special ability (Dead of Winter style). Some are
    overpowered, some are weak; the game balances overall.
  - Personalities can override your orders (panic, rage, greed).
  - Experience comes from kills and actions; skills grow from use.
  - People carry gear, armour and drugs (temporary buffs).
  - You can recruit and grow the group between missions.
  - You can rename people (defaults are kept if you don't).
  - Permadeath, one save. If everyone dies, it's game over.

Fights
  - Move + 1 action per person per turn.
  - Attacks can miss, based on who is attacking (their stats).
  - XCOM-style directional cover. Trees are cover, water matters.
  - No telegraphed enemy intent: surprise matters.
  - Ammo is scarce, and gunfire draws zombies. Stealth is optional: people can
    hide in houses and woods, but getting attacked first is bad.
  - Houses are 2x2 tiles, so up to 4 people fit. Doors can be barricaded.
  - Missions have different goals: get through, take a bridge, hold out, and
    so on. Not just "kill everything".
  - You can scavenge inside missions.
  - Retreat is allowed.
  - Bitten = infected: the person turns into a zombie a few days later
    unless treated.

Journey
  - "The Last Boat": reach Haven Bay before the last ship sails.
  - Food, fuel and resources drain on a clock. Random events happen.
  - Scavenge between missions too.
  - At each stop you choose ONE thing to do.
  - A caravan of several vehicles that you upgrade. Party size = seats.
  - No vehicles on the tactical map.

Other
  - Difficulty modes, Normal by default.
  - Searching houses never backfires by itself.
  - Story choices are locked in before you see the outcome (a power cut
    can't undo them).
  - Later: zombie dogs, bears and bigger, scarier monsters.


================================================================================
3. THE WORLD AND THE STORY
================================================================================

SETTING
  Cedar Falls, a small river town in a forgotten corner of the Pacific
  Northwest, in late autumn. Pine forest, farmland, logging roads, a dead
  highway and a cold grey coast. It rains a lot. Nights are long and get
  longer as the trip goes on.

THE OUTBREAK
  It started at St. Agnes hospital in Cedar Falls. A man was brought in from
  a logging road with bite marks nobody could explain. He died on the table
  and got up again. The staff called him Patient Zero. Within a week the town
  was gone.

  The dead are slow alone and deadly in numbers. They follow noise. They are
  drawn to the living. Something about Patient Zero is different: the dead
  near him move together, like one animal. He is somewhere behind you, and
  the horde is following.

THE GOAL
  A radio message on a military channel: an evacuation ship, the last one,
  leaves Haven Bay on the coast. Day 40 is the last day. After that, nobody
  is coming. Haven Bay is about 12 stops away.

THE GROUP
  It starts with four: Mara (a firefighter), Doc (an ER doctor from St.
  Agnes), June (a young radio operator who heard the message) and Holt (an
  ex-army sergeant). Everyone else is met on the road.

THREE ACTS

  ACT 1: LEAVING CEDAR FALLS (Days 1-12)        [the demo covers this act]
    Stops: Cedar Falls -> Main Street or the Gas Station -> Hensley Farm or
    the County Highway -> Miller's Bridge.
    Theme: panic and first losses. The group learns to scavenge, to be quiet,
    and that guns are a last resort. Ends crossing the river at Miller's
    Bridge.
    Story beats: the first bite in the group; Doc's guilt about Patient Zero
    ("I signed the transfer papers"); June keeps the radio alive; first
    recruits (Rosa the mechanic at the gas station, Tess the runaway on Main
    Street).

  ACT 2: THE DEAD COUNTRY (Days 13-28)
    Stops: the forest highway, a logging camp, a flooded valley (water
    missions), a burned-out outpost, Ridgeview (a dead city with a hospital
    and a pharmacy district), a prison bus, a church refuge.
    Theme: the group becomes a community, and the road starts to cost people.
    Trading outposts appear. Other survivors are not always friendly.
    Story beats: Cole, a stranger who survived a bite, joins (the mystery);
    a church refuge that wants your people to stay; a decision at Ridgeview
    hospital (antivirals vs. saving a child); the horde front catches up for
    the first time (a siege).

  ACT 3: THE COAST (Days 29-40)
    Stops: the coast road, a lighthouse, the fish cannery, the harbour
    district of Haven Bay.
    Theme: the clock. Too few seats, too little fuel, and Patient Zero is
    close. The boat is real, and it might not wait.
    Story beats: Cole's secret (he is immune, and Patient Zero is drawn to
    him); the final run through Haven Bay to the dock; Patient Zero, the
    boss, at the harbour.

ENDINGS (based on what happened, not chosen from a menu)
  - The Last Boat: you reach the dock before it sails. The ending lists who
    made it and who didn't, with their Chronicle epitaphs.
  - Too Late: the boat sails without you. The survivors watch from the pier.
    "Nobody is coming."
  - Alone: one person makes it. A quiet, bitter ending.
  - Cole's choice (secret): if Cole is alive and trusted, he can stay behind
    on the pier to draw the horde away from the boat.
  - Game over: everyone dies. The memorial shows every grave.

PATIENT ZERO (the antagonist)
  - Seen in flashbacks and at a distance in Act 1. Appears in Act 2 sieges.
    Fought in Act 3.
  - Boss rules (designed, not built yet):
    * the dead near him hit harder and stand firmer;
    * he summons more dead at night;
    * he heals while nests survive;
    * people he kills rise faster;
    * at half health he goes into a frenzy;
    * when he dies, every zombie on the map takes heavy damage ("the link is
      broken").

4. HOW THE STORY EVOLVES
================================================================================

The story is mostly made by what happens to YOUR group, plus a few fixed
beats (the boat, Patient Zero, Cole).

STORYLETS
  Small scenes, 2-6 lines long, often with a choice. They fire at set
  moments:
    - at camp (every night)
    - on the road (between stops)
    - at a stop (arriving)
    - before and after missions
    - when someone dies
    - when someone is bitten

  Each storylet has conditions, for example: "two living people who trust
  each other, one of them infected, meds below 2, at night". The game picks
  one that fits the current state.

  Storylets refer to ROLES, not names: "a Caring person", "someone infected",
  "Doc (if alive)". The game fills in whoever fits. A dead person can never
  speak, because they can't fill a role.

  Effects are small and meaningful: supplies +/- 1-3, morale, relationships,
  a trait gained or lost, rarely an injury or a death.

RELATIONSHIPS
  Every pair of people has a value from -3 (enemies) to +3 (together).
  Storylets change it. Bonded friends fight better side by side and grieve
  hard. Rivals argue at camp.

PERMADEATH
  Dead is dead. The roster keeps a grave with an epitaph. Later storylets
  remember the dead ("Mara still sets out five bowls").

THE CAMPFIRE CHRONICLE
  A journal of the run: who joined, who died and how, bites, amputations,
  kills, missions, choices. Shown at camp, at game over and at the ending.
  Later, a web page will read the save file from the SD card and print the
  run as a story.

WHAT A WRITER DELIVERS
  - Storylets as rows in tables (see deadwars/docs/tech/STORY.md in the
    repo).
  - Lines must fit the dialogue box: about 23 characters x 5 lines with a
    portrait, 28 x 5 without.
  - Every name in text is a placeholder like {A.name}, never typed out.
  - Voice: short, plain, tired, human. Dark humour is welcome; melodrama is
    not.
  - Example:
      A: "You ate my share."
      B: "I was hungry. We're all hungry."
      Choice: Let it go (morale -1) / Make them pay it back (food +1,
      relationship -1).

================================================================================
6. THE JOURNEY (the Organ Trail layer)
================================================================================

THE ROUTE MAP
  A branching road map, like FTL. At each stop you see 2-3 next stops. Each
  shows its name, its type (town, farm, gas station, outpost, hospital,
  bridge...), the fuel cost, the days of travel and a one-line rumour.

  The horde front (Patient Zero's horde) follows you on the map and moves
  forward every day. Resting and detours let it catch up. A stop it reaches
  becomes a siege.

AT EACH STOP, CHOOSE ONE
  Scavenge  play the stop's mission. Risk people, gain supplies, open the
            road.
  Bypass    pay extra fuel and/or a day; no fight, no loot.
  Trade /   outposts only: swap supplies, hire people, buy or repair
  Recruit   vehicles and upgrades.
  Rest      heal people, raise morale, treat infection. Costs food and a
            day, risks a night event, and lets the horde catch up.

ROAD EVENTS (between stops)
  Breakdown, roadblock, a stranger flagging you down, a trade caravan, a
  horde crossing the road, a gas station, a body with a backpack, an infected
  hitchhiker, a radio message about the boat, a bandit toll. Each is a
  storylet with choices, often decided by stats ("Rosa can fix it: Mechanics
  3 needed").

PACE AND RATIONS
  Pace:    Careful (slower, safer) / Steady / Hard driving (faster, more
           breakdowns and exhaustion).
  Rations: Full / Half (less morale, Grit checks) / None (people starve).


================================================================================
7. THE CARAVAN, RESOURCES AND THE CLOCK
================================================================================

RESOURCES (all drain together; this is the clock)
  Food    1 per person per day.
  Fuel    per vehicle per leg (sedan 2, van 3, bus 5). Run out: abandon a
          vehicle and anyone who no longer fits.
  Ammo    shared pool, scarce, spent per shot.
  Meds    healing, treating infection.
  Parts   repairs, barricades, upgrades, crafting.
  Morale  0-10; low morale makes people panic and fight.
  Days    every leg, rest and mission costs time. The boat leaves on Day 40.

  Demo (Act 1 only) starting supplies on Normal: food 24, fuel 12, ammo 20,
  meds 4, parts 4. The demo's boat deadline is scaled to the slice: Day 12 /
  10 / 9 / 8 (Easy / Normal / Hard / Very Hard).

VEHICLES (the caravan)
  Party size = total seats. Loot beyond the cargo space must be left behind.
  Sedan     4 seats, 2 cargo, fuel 2
  Pickup    2 seats, 6 cargo, fuel 2
  Van       6 seats, 3 cargo, fuel 3
  School bus 12 seats, 4 cargo, fuel 5
  Motorbike 1 seat, fuel 1, can scout ahead
  RV        4 seats, beds (heal at camp), fuel 4
  Each has a condition (breakdowns get likelier below 50%) and up to 2
  upgrades: plow plating, roof rack, extra tank, CB radio, sleeping cots.
  The demo starts with a sedan and a pickup.


================================================================================
8. CAMP (every night)
================================================================================

Give each person one job:
  Cook     saves food, raises morale (Survival skill)
  Guard    prevents or weakens night raids (Nerve)
  Repair   fixes vehicles (Mechanics)
  Tend     heals the wounded, treats infection, amputates (Medicine)
  Forage   a quick, risky scavenge for food, parts or fuel (Scavenging,
           Stealth)
  Scout    reveals details of the next stops
  Rest     heals
  Craft    molotovs, barricade kits, drugs (Bianca)
Then storylets play: arguments, confessions, romances, theft, a fever
breaking, someone who wants to leave.


================================================================================
9. MISSIONS (the tactical layer)
================================================================================

SCALE
  One tile = one person (16 x 16 pixels). Maps are up to 32 x 32 tiles; the
  screen shows 15 x 10 tiles. Houses are 2 x 2 tiles (4 people), shops and
  hospitals are bigger.

MISSION TYPES
  Scavenge run       loot what you can, then get out. Noise brings waves.
  Get through        get at least N people to the far side.
  Take the bridge    clear the bridge, then hold it for N turns.
  Hold out           survive N turns, usually at night, in a barricaded
                     building.
  Rescue             find hidden survivors in buildings and escort them out;
                     they join you.
  Retrieve           get one item (antivirals for a bitten friend, a radio
                     part, keys), then leave.
  Defend the caravan keep the dead off the vehicles while they're repaired.
  Burn the nest      reach and burn a nest.
  Siege              the horde caught you: break out or hold out.
  Boss               Patient Zero (Act 3).
  Missions also have 0-2 optional goals: a cache, a stranded survivor, a
  vehicle to hot-wire, a fuel truck to drain.

MODIFIERS
  Night, Rain (quieter, shorter sight), Fog, Dense horde, Sleeping horde,
  Low on ammo, an infected friend in the team, a panicky person to escort.

DEPLOY, RETREAT, RESULTS
  Choose who goes (4-6). People standing on the caravan or exit tiles can
  leave at any time. Anyone left on the map when everyone else leaves is lost.
  Failing a mission costs what it was for (no bridge = a detour), but never
  ends the game by itself.

THE DEMO'S THREE MAPS
  Main Street      (scavenge run, day)  houses, a pharmacy, a shop, a garage,
                   wrecked cars with trunks to search.
  Hensley Farm     (get through, night) farmhouse, barn, fences, fields.
  Miller's Bridge  (take the bridge, day) a river, the bridge, both banks.


================================================================================
10. COMBAT, COVER, NOISE AND STEALTH
================================================================================

A TURN
  Each person gets a Move and 1 Action (either order). Actions: attack,
  search, barricade a door, heal, use an item, overwatch, help a downed
  friend, leave the map, wait. Then the dead move.

HIT CHANCE (attacks can miss; who attacks matters)
  Ranged:  50 + 8 x Aim + 5 x Firearms + weapon accuracy
           - cover - range penalty - night penalty
           + flank bonus + height bonus
  Melee:   60 + 6 x Speed + 5 x Melee + weapon accuracy
           + bonus vs. an unaware target
  Always between 5% and 95%. The screen shows the hit % and the damage
  before you commit.

DIRECTIONAL COVER (XCOM style)
  Cover sits on the edges of a tile: a fence, wall, wreck, tree or building
  wall next to you. Half cover -20%, full cover -40%, only against attacks
  from that side. A zombie coming round the side = FLANKED (full damage,
  +15% to hit). Melee ignores cover, but a barricaded door blocks it both
  ways.

NOISE AND THE HORDE METER
  Every action makes noise (knife 1, bat 2, pistol 6, shotgun 9, rifle 10,
  breaking a door 4, barricading 3). Zombies within range come to look. Noise
  also fills the mission's Horde meter; at set levels, waves of the dead
  arrive from the map edges. The meter also rises a little every turn, and
  faster on harder difficulties.

STEALTH
  A person in woods or inside a building who hasn't made a loud noise is
  hidden; the dead don't target them unless they're right next to them.
  Attacking something that doesn't know you're there = +25% to hit and +1
  damage. The dead get the same bonus against you (ambushes in dark houses).

BUILDINGS
  2 x 2 tiles, one person per tile. No interior view: the roof stays drawn,
  with small portrait badges showing who is inside. Doors can be barricaded
  (1 action, 1 part). The dead must break the barricade, which takes time
  and makes noise. Searching each tile is silent and never springs a trap,
  but sleeping dead can be inside.

WATER
  The dead can't swim, so rivers are defensive lines. (Wading for people is
  planned.)


================================================================================
11. PEOPLE: ATTRIBUTES, SKILLS, PERSONALITY, INFECTION
================================================================================

ATTRIBUTES (1-5)
  Grit  max HP (6 + Grit), resists infection, hunger and cold
  Aim   ranged accuracy
  Speed movement (3-5 tiles) and dodging
  Nerve resists panic; night penalties

SKILLS (0-5, grow from use)
  Firearms, Melee, Medicine, Mechanics, Stealth, Scavenging, Survival,
  Leadership. Shooting trains Firearms, searching trains Scavenging, and so
  on.

PERSONALITY (2 traits each, about 20 traits)
  Brave, Cowardly, Hothead, Calm, Loner, Loyal, Caring, Selfish, Greedy,
  Reckless, Paranoid, Optimist, Cynic, Devout, Addict, Haunted, Stubborn,
  Curious, Fragile, Glutton, Pacifist, Clever, Weary.
  Traits give small stat quirks, steer the storylets, and can OVERRIDE your
  orders after a failed Nerve check (a friend dies, someone is bitten, being
  surrounded, night, starving):
    Flee (Cowardly), Freeze (Fragile), Berserk (Hothead/Reckless),
    Grab loot or drugs (Greedy/Addict), Refuse to leave a friend (Loyal).

GEAR
  A weapon, armour, and 2 pack slots.
  Weapons: knife, bat, machete, axe, crossbow, pistol, shotgun, hunting
  rifle, molotov.
  Armour: leather jacket (fewer infections), riot gear (far fewer, slower).
  Drugs (temporary buffs, risk of addiction): adrenaline, painkillers,
  stims, sedatives, antivirals.

INJURY, BITES AND DEATH
  0 HP -> downed: bleeding out for 2 turns. A friend can stabilise or carry
  them; otherwise they die. People killed by the dead rise as zombies.
  Bitten -> INFECTED: they turn in about 3 days (more with Grit, fewer on
  Hard). Options at camp: amputate (cured, but a permanent penalty), spend
  antivirals (buys time), quarantine, or a mercy scene.


================================================================================
12. THE ZOMBIES (and what comes later)
================================================================================

NOW
  Walker   slow, common, the backbone of the horde.
  Runner   fast and fragile; arrives first.
  Brute    huge and tough; breaks barricades in one hit.
  Spitter  spits acid from range 2 (acts like a Walker in the demo).
  Bloater  bursts when killed and hurts everything next to it.
  One zombie per tile. States: asleep -> alerted by noise or sight ->
  hunting -> attacking.

LATER
  Zombie dogs: fast, hunt by smell, ignore hiding.
  Bears: rare, terrifying, neutral until provoked.
  Big monsters: giant Brute variants; Patient Zero's "Hive".

  turns                                                antivirals
  Events                soft      standard  harsh     brutal
  Always one save slot (ironman): it saves at every stop and every story
  choice.


================================================================================
15. ART DIRECTION
================================================================================

MOOD
  Gritty 16-bit horror. Desaturated greens and browns by day, deep blue
  nights, blood red as the only loud colour. Rain, mud, rust, wet asphalt,
  pine forests, grey coast. Think "Advance Wars 2 sprites drawn by someone
  who has seen The Walking Dead": readable and chunky, but grim.

READABILITY RULES (the screen is tiny)
  - Silhouettes first. Every person must be recognisable at 16 x 16 by shape
    and one or two colours (Mara's yellow stripe, Doc's white coat, June's
    blue jacket, Holt's olive and rifle, Rosa's red bandana, Tess's grey
    hood).
  - A 1-pixel dark outline round every sprite.
  - Light from the top-left.
  - No soft gradients, no anti-aliasing, no transparency effects.
  - Survivors lean blue/warm, the dead lean sickly green/grey with red.

HARDWARE LIMITS (the GBA)
  - Each sprite uses ONE palette of 15 colours + transparent. Survivors share
    3 palettes, zombies share 1, so keep colours consistent across
    characters.
  - Backgrounds are built from 8 x 8 tiles; map tiles are 16 x 16.
  - Portraits: one palette each (15 colours).
  - Full-screen pictures (title, journey map): one 16-colour palette, at most
    about 500 unique 8 x 8 tiles (simple, repeating areas help).


================================================================================
16. ART ASSET LIST, CHATGPT PROMPTS, AND HOW TO HAND ART BACK
================================================================================

HOW TO WORK WITH CHATGPT IMAGES
  ChatGPT makes large, painterly images. That's fine: use them as CONCEPT
  ART and REFERENCE. I'll convert them to exact GBA pixel art (the right
  size, palette and outline), keeping the design. For the best results:

  1. Ask for "pixel art", "limited palette", "flat colours, no
     anti-aliasing", "plain flat background" (or transparent), "front view"
     or "side view facing right" as noted below.
  2. One character or object per image, centred, full body, nothing cropped.
  3. For sprite sheets, ask for the poses side by side on one sheet.
  4. Keep the same character's colours across all images (paste the colour
     notes from section 5 into each prompt).
  5. Send me the PNGs as they are, and name the files like
     mara_concept.png, mara_portrait.png, roof_house.png, journey_map.png.
     Add a line saying what each one is for.

  A base style line to put at the start of every prompt:
    "16-bit pixel art in the style of Game Boy Advance tactics games
     (Advance Wars 2), gritty zombie horror, muted palette, dark 1-pixel
     outlines, light from the top-left, flat colours, no anti-aliasing, no
     text."

PRIORITY 1: THE DEMO CAST (6 characters)
  For each of Mara, Doc, June, Holt, Rosa, Tess, make:

  a) Concept sheet (reference)
     Prompt: "<base style> Character concept sheet for <NAME>, <LOOK from
     section 5>. Full body, front view and side view facing right, plus a
     close-up of the face. Plain flat grey background."

  b) Portrait (in game: 32 x 32 pixels, shown in dialogue boxes)
     Prompt: "<base style> Pixel art portrait, head and shoulders, of <NAME>,
     <LOOK>. Square image, facing slightly right, simple dark background.
     Readable at very small size; strong shapes."
     Final size in game: 32 x 32, 15 colours.

  c) Map sprite (in game: 16 x 16 pixels, 2 frames)
     Prompt: "<base style> Tiny 16x16 pixel art sprite sheet of <NAME>, side
     view facing right, full body, 2 frames: standing and walking. Very
     chunky, readable silhouette. Transparent background."

  d) Battle figure (in game: 32 x 32 pixels, 4 frames)
     Prompt: "<base style> 32x32 pixel art sprite sheet of <NAME> facing
     right with <WEAPON>, 4 frames: idle 1, idle 2, wind-up, attack. Same
     character as the portrait. Transparent background."

PRIORITY 2: THE DEAD (5 types)
  Walker, Runner, Brute, Spitter, Bloater. Same three outputs as the people:
  concept sheet, 16x16 map sprite (2 frames, facing right), 32x32 battle
  figure (4 frames).
  Prompt: "<base style> Zombie: <TYPE DESCRIPTION>. Sickly green-grey skin,
  torn clothes, red wounds. Facing right."
  Type descriptions:
    Walker:  slumped, arms forward, torn shirt.
    Runner:  thin, leaning forward mid-sprint, red-stained clothes.
    Brute:   huge, swollen, hunched, tiny head, broken chains.
    Spitter: hunched, distended jaw, glowing green acid dripping.
    Bloater: grotesquely swollen purple body, pustules.
  Also: Patient Zero (boss): "tall gaunt man in a torn hospital gown, IV
  tube still in his arm, pale glowing eyes, the dead around him leaning
  toward him".

PRIORITY 3: BUILDINGS (seen from above, 16 x 16 tiles)
  Roofs for houses, a shop, a garage, a pharmacy, a hospital, a barn, a
  church. Buildings are 2 x 2 or 3 x 2 tiles (32 x 32 or 48 x 32 pixels).
  Prompt: "<base style> Top-down 3/4 view pixel art of a small <BUILDING>
  roof for a tactics game map, 32x32 pixels (2x2 tiles of 16x16), clear
  roof edges, a door on the bottom edge, matches a grassy town map.
  Transparent background."
  Plus small icons (16 x 16): a door, a barricaded door (planks), a backpack
  cache, an exit flag, a hidden eye, infected (a drop), downed (a red
  cross), a noise ring.

PRIORITY 4: MAP TERRAIN (16 x 16 tiles that repeat)
  Grass, woods, rubble, river, bridge, road (with corners and crossings),
  fence, wrecked car, ruins, mud, a cornfield, a pier, sand, rocks. Night
  versions are made automatically.
  Prompt: "<base style> Seamless tileable 16x16 pixel art terrain tile of
  <TERRAIN> for a top-down tactics map, gritty and muted."

PRIORITY 5: SCREENS (full screen, 240 x 160 pixels)
  - Title screen: "Dead Wars: The Last Boat". A ruined Pacific Northwest
    town at night, a distant ship's light on the sea, a crowd of the dead in
    silhouette. Leave the bottom 5 rows of text (about 40 px) dark for the
    menu. I add the logo text myself.
  - Journey map: a dark, hand-drawn road map from Cedar Falls (left) to Haven
    Bay (right) across forest, farmland, a river, a dead city, and the coast.
    Leave the top 16 px and bottom 40 px plain for text.
  - Battle backdrops: 120 x 104 px panels for plain, woods, ruins, city,
    nest, farm and coast.
  - Camp scene: a campfire at night with the vehicles parked around it.
  Prompt: "<base style> Full-screen 240x160 pixel art illustration of
  <SCENE>. Limited 16-colour palette, simple large shapes, no text."

PRIORITY 6: LATER
  The other 18 cast members (section 5), vehicles (for the caravan screen),
  zombie dogs, bears, big monsters, story illustrations (key moments: the
  boat, the pier, Patient Zero).

WHAT I DO WITH YOUR IMAGES
  I resize, re-palette and re-outline each image into the exact GBA format,
  then put it in the game and send you screenshots and a new build to check
  on the Miyoo. If an image doesn't survive the shrink (too much detail), I
  will tell you which parts to simplify.

17. AUDIO DIRECTION
================================================================================

  - Music in layers that follow the Horde meter: quiet ambience when calm, a
    pulse at the first threshold, full dread when the waves come.
  - A fragile, warm camp theme; a lonely road theme.
  - Sharp noise effects: a gunshot echo, glass, a door splintering, a moan.
  - A short death sting for each character.
  - The GBA sound chip is simple: think chiptune with lots of silence.

19. GLOSSARY
================================================================================

  Storylet       a small story scene with conditions, choices and effects.
  Chronicle      the run's journal.
  Horde meter    noise in a mission; when it fills, waves of the dead come.
  Flanked        attacked from a side with no cover.
  Unaware        a target that hasn't noticed the attacker (+25% to hit).
  Downed         at 0 HP, bleeding out for 2 turns.
  Infected       bitten; turns into a zombie after a few days.
  Ironman        one save slot; you can't reload a bad outcome.
  Horde front    Patient Zero's horde chasing you on the route map.
  Tile           one 16 x 16 square on the map; one person per tile.
